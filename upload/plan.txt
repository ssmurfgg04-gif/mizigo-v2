Yes. The key is not to build “Uber, but with trucks.” Build the marketplace + dispatch system for cargo, using the parts of Uber/Faras that make booking frictionless, then add the things normal ride-hailing apps do not solve: weight, dimensions, loading, vehicle capacity, cargo type, delivery proof, negotiated/market pricing, and business accounts.

I studied the current Uber/FARAS patterns and Uber Freight model for this. Uber emphasizes destination-first booking, suggested pickup points, upfront pricing, driver matching, live arrival tracking, trip identity, ratings and receipts. 
 FARAS combines ride-hailing with delivery, payments, rewards and local-market mobility. 
 Uber Freight adds the crucial freight layer: shipment details, equipment type, dimensions/weight, quote comparison, booking, real-time visibility and document management. 

For Kenya, M-Pesa should be a first-class payment method rather than an afterthought; Safaricom's current Daraja platform supports C2B, B2C, B2B and M-Pesa Express integrations.

One small but important correction to our earlier plan: Taste Skill is excellent, but it's primarily a frontend/design skill, not a complete software-engineering discipline. The current repo itself describes its focus around frontend design, while its different variants target things like GPT/Codex, image-to-code and redesigns. 

So I deliberately made the builder research and install complementary engineering skills rather than pretending Taste Skill covers backend, security, payments, distributed systems, testing, etc.

And I'd specifically tell it to use the current v2 rather than blindly using an old Taste Skill prompt; the repository says v2 is now the default and is a substantial rewrite, while v1 is retained for compatibility. 

That gives you a much better sequence:

your entire chat → skill acquisition → research/architecture → design system → vertical implementation → testing → audits → MVP.

Not:

your entire chat → “generate me an Uber clone” → 150 pretty screens.
